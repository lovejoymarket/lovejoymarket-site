Chapter One: The Artist

I’m Jessie. I tattoo, paint, draw, print, build things, and occasionally turn one idea into five more because apparently that is my process.

Art came first. Tattooing gave all of it somewhere to land. The drawing matters, obviously, but so does the way it fits your body and whether you feel comfortable enough to tell me when something is not right.

I like black and gray, bold contrast, clean shapes, open skin, and work that still reads from across the room. I also like clients with an actual point of view. Convenient.

Over the last few years, I built the kind of tattoo practice I wanted to walk into: personal, relaxed, honest, and serious about the work without acting painfully serious about itself.

Chapter Two: The Market

LoveJoy Market happened because I got tired of pretending every part of my creative life needed its own little box.

I wanted the tattoo studio, art on the walls, people selling what they made, music, classes, and the conversation after everybody was technically supposed to leave in the same place.

“Market” means more than shopping here. You might come for a tattoo and leave with a print, a new artist to follow, or plans for next weekend. That is the good part.

The Fishers space lets me do it for real. It is a tattoo studio first, with room for art, pop-ups, classes, movie nights, guest artists, and any other idea good enough to earn a yes.

The Point, Basically

I am not trying to turn LoveJoy into some polished, faceless creative empire. If it ever stops feeling like a person made it, I did something wrong.

I want to make strong tattoos, show good art, give artists fair opportunities, and throw events worth leaving the house for. I also want enough room to follow a weird idea before it has a business plan.

The point is not to do everything. It is to let the right things live together without sanding off the personality.
