Chapter One: The Artist

I am Jessica LoveJoy. I make things, then I usually make a system, a room, or an event around the thing because apparently simply having one interest was never going to happen.

I came to tattooing through visual art, not the other way around. Drawing, painting, design, printmaking, photography, and whatever else I can get my hands on all feed the same instinct: make the image stronger, make the idea clearer, make the experience feel intentional.

Tattooing became the center of that practice because it asks for all of it at once. Taste. Trust. Technical discipline. Reading a person correctly. Knowing when to push an idea and when to get out of its way.

I have spent the last several years building a tattoo practice that looks like me: high-contrast, graphic, thoughtful, a little off-center, and very uninterested in pretending personality is unprofessional.

Chapter Two: The Market

LoveJoy Market grew out of a simple problem: I kept wanting the different parts of my creative life to stop acting like they needed separate addresses.

The tattoo studio, the art on the walls, the people selling things they made, the DJ, the movie night, the weird little class, the conversation after everyone was technically supposed to go home. Those things belong together.

So the “Market” in LoveJoy Market is not just retail. It means exchange. Work moving between people. Ideas moving between people. Artists getting seen. Someone coming in for a tattoo and accidentally finding a print they love, an event they want to attend, or a person whose work they had never seen before.

The Fishers space became the physical version of that idea: a tattoo studio first, but also a small gallery, a rotating home for featured artists, a place for pop-ups, classes, movie nights, makers, guest tattooers, and whatever else survives the question, “would this be cool here?”

The Point, Basically

I do not want LoveJoy Market to become a giant anonymous “creative brand.” I want it to stay specific enough that you can feel who made it.

That means good tattoos. Good art. Fair opportunities for artists. Events people actually want to attend. A space where local creative work is treated like culture instead of decoration. And enough flexibility to keep making the next weird thing before there is a tidy category for it.

The goal is not to be everything. The goal is to make the right things feel at home together.
